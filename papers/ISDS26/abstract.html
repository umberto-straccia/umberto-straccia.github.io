Thai Le, Thanh Ma, Phu-An Thai, Quoc-Khang Tran, Cang Dang, Long Huynh, Bui Xuan Tung and Umberto Straccia.

PoleXAI: An Explainable Neuro-Symbolic Framework for Visual Damage Assessment of Telecommunication Poles.

In Proceedings of the International Conference on Intelligent Systems and Data Science (ISDS-26),  Communications in Computer and Information Science, Volume 3253, pages 370-385, 2026.

Abstract:

Visual assessment of telecommunication poles requires detecting surface defects, quantifying their spatial extent relative to the visible pole structure, and translating visual evidence into actionable maintenance guidance in a form that field operators can verify. Purely data-driven approaches address detection well but provide no mechanism for inspecting the reasoning that connects a pixel-level
observation to a maintenance decision. We propose PoleXAI, a neuro-symbolic framework that couples deep visual perception with explicit symbolic reasoning to produce an auditable inspection output. Instance segmentation localizes visible damage regions; a lightweight convolutional classifier distinguishes four degradation types; a pole-relative quantification step measures the affected
fraction of the visible pole area; and a defect-specific fuzzy inference stage maps the resulting percentage to a continuous severity score, which a constraint-based layer then resolves to a discrete severity level and a maintenance recommendation. Every intermediate value, from damage and pole masks to fuzzy score and severity, is exposed to the operator rather than collapsed into
a single opaque label. Evaluated on a field dataset of Mekong Delta infrastructure images, the YOLO11l segmentation model achieves a Mask mAP50 of 0.408, and a MobileNet classifier attains 98.53% aggregate accuracy on four damage categories. The results demonstrate that integrating deep visual perception with explicit symbolic rules yields an inspection system whose decisions are both accurate and traceable.
